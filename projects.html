Real-Time Task-Space Impedance Control, CRS Arm hardware

Bare-metal C · TI C2000 TMS320F28335 · 3-joint CRS arm · Spring 2026 (ME 446)

A 1 kHz impedance controller in C that makes a 3-joint arm behave like a programmable spring and damper in Cartesian space, with stiffness that changes per waypoint. Validated on the arm with peg-in-hole insertion and compliant contour following along a zig-zag path.

Control law

x, ẋ   = f(q), J(q) q̇                  forward kinematics
F      = Kp (x_d − x) + Kd (ẋ_d − ẋ)       Kp, Kd diagonal, set per waypoint
τ      = J(q)ᵀ F + τ_fric(q̇)
τ_fric = b ⊙ q̇ + c ⊙ sign(q̇)             viscous + Coulomb, per joint
τ_i    = clamp(τ_i, −5, +5) N·m

Each waypoint carries its own stiffness mode. Making some Cartesian axes soft and others stiff gives selective compliance: the arm yields where contact is expected and holds position elsewhere.

Engineering details

  • Joint velocity from finite-differenced encoder angles through a 3-sample moving average, inside the 1 ms loop.
  • Normal force against a surface is open-loop: commanded through an estimated contact stiffness (no force sensor), and checked against a physical scale.
  • Debugged actuator saturation, loop-timing overruns and near-singular configurations on an oscilloscope.

Code is course lab work and is not public (available on request). No logged trajectory plots are published yet.

Autonomous Driving Stack, Polaris GEM e4 in progress

Python · C++ · ROS 2 · OpenCV · four-person team, ECE 484 · Oct 2026 to present

A ROS 2 autonomy stack for a full-scale Ackermann-steered electric vehicle: follow the lane along a recorded route, stop for signs, pedestrians and obstacles, resume, and stop at the goal. I own dynamics, controls and testing.

Status, stated precisely. On the vehicle: camera-based lane keeping on straights and through turns. In simulation: full laps in our track simulator. Not yet demonstrated on the vehicle: live sign, pedestrian and obstacle stops and start-to-goal runs (scheduled for the December 11 demo).

My part: control and test tooling

  • Path tracking: Pure Pursuit with speed-scaled lookahead, and Stanley as a comparison.
  • Curvature-based corner-speed planner with braking-distance deceleration into stops.
  • Speed PID on throttle and brake; waypoint progression along the route.
  • Command limiter: steering angle, steering rate and acceleration clamped before the PACMod drive-by-wire.
  • Test loop: a track simulator and a Gazebo world built from ROS bags and the RTK route recorded on the car; vehicle calibration (camera tilt, sensor offsets, wheelbase, lane width) from recorded bags.

Built with the team

Bird's-eye lane fit (primary steering reference, GNSS/INS waypoints as fallback), YOLO11n stop-sign and pedestrian detection, LiDAR corridor obstacle detection, and the DRIVE / STOP-SIGN HOLD / OBSTACLE HOLD / GOAL state machine.

Path tracking

Pure Pursuit (rear axle, wheelbase L, lookahead l_d growing with speed):
  δ = atan( 2 L sin α / l_d )
Stanley (front axle, cross-track e, heading error ψ_e):
  δ = ψ_e + atan( k e / (k_s + v) )
Speed: u = Kp e_v + Ki ∫e_v + Kd ė_v  → throttle / brake
Limits: |δ| ≤ δ_max, |δ̇| ≤ δ̇_max, |a| ≤ a_max

UR3 Kinematics and Robotic Drawing hardware

Python · ROS · OpenCV · ECE 470 · Jan to May 2026

Give the arm an image; it extracts contours, maps them to a 20 cm square on the table, and draws them with a pen on a physical 6-DOF UR3, using inverse kinematics solved in closed form. Classical vision and analytic motion planning, no learning. Drew a 142-stroke test image in 7 minutes on hardware.

Kinematics

Forward (product of exponentials):
  T(θ) = e^[S1]θ1 e^[S2]θ2 ··· e^[S6]θ6 M,   S_i = (ω_i, −ω_i × q_i)
Inverse (closed form, elbow-up):
  wrist centre from the pen tip and yaw
  θ1 from base geometry, arcsin correction for the shoulder offset
  θ2, θ3 from the two-link triangle (law of cosines)
  θ4 keeps the tool vertical,  θ5 = −90°,  θ6 from yaw
  check: T(θ) recomputed by FK before sending

Vision to motion

image → grayscale + Gaussian blur → Canny (50, 150)
      → findContours + approxPolyDP (0.2% of arc length)
      → drop strokes < 50 px → pixels to metres (20 cm square)
per stroke: pen-up MoveJ to start, pen-down MoveL through
            every waypoint, pen-up at the end

Joint targets go out on ur3/command. The program waits until every joint's feedback on ur3/position is within 0.0005 rad of its target before sending the next waypoint: that number is the per-joint arrival threshold in the code, not a separately measured tracking error.

Source code The repository README lists the known limitations as they ran on hardware.

Underactuated Balance Control

MATLAB · Simulink · Rust · Jan 2025 to Jun 2026

Reaction-wheel inverted pendulum hardware

Pole-placement state feedback with a Luenberger observer, built as two decoupled 2-state blocks and gain-scheduled between the upright and hanging linearizations. Friction parameters identified from logged step-response data. Measured settling time 1.1 s on the rig.

ẋ = A_s x + B_s u,  y = C x        s ∈ {upright, hanging}
u = −K_s x̂,   eig(A_s − B_s K_s) at the chosen poles
x̂̇ = A_s x̂ + B_s u + L_s (y − C x̂),   observer poles faster
switch (A_s, B_s, K_s, L_s) by pendulum angle

Cart-pole control simulator simulation

A Rust simulator of the full nonlinear cart-pole (Lagrangian equations of motion, RK4 at 1 kHz with zero-order hold on the control) with a from-scratch LQR: numerical linearization about upright, discretization, and the discrete Riccati equation solved by fixed-point iteration. Compared against PID on pole angle.

P ← Q + Aᵀ P A − Aᵀ P B (R + Bᵀ P B)⁻¹ Bᵀ P A      (iterate to convergence)
K = (R + Bᵀ P B)⁻¹ Bᵀ P A,    u = −K x,   |u| ≤ 25 N

AI Smart Glasses for Hands-Free Datasheet Retrieval ongoing

C · ESP-IDF · ESP32-S3 · OV5640 · custom PCB · ECE 445 senior design, three-person team · Sep 2026 to present

An end-to-end embedded AI wearable: look at an electronic component, press a button, and the glasses identify it and display its datasheet in about 1 s. Built on a custom ESP32-S3 board with an OV5640 camera, designed from scratch with no development boards.

My part: embedded firmware

  • Camera capture over the OV5640's 8-bit DVP interface, with autofocus (1.5 s focus deadline) and work lights during exposure.
  • Communication: Wi-Fi upload to the vision server with a shared token, buffer-until-ACK, timeouts and retries.
  • Button debounce, status LED, battery monitoring, and timestamped event logs per transaction.
  • Integration with the team's computer vision and object-tracking pipeline, which identifies the component and returns its datasheet.

Data flow

button → autofocus → OV5640 JPEG capture (ESP32-S3)
       → Wi-Fi upload (token, ACK, retry)
       → vision server: detect + track component → identify part
       → datasheet lookup → shown to the wearer      ≈ 1 s
